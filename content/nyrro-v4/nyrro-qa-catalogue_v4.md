## Part 2 — The 5 case studies

### Intelligent Enterprise Search Platform

**Recognised by:** enterprise search, unify search, unifyapps search, search case study, agent marketplace, first case study, 1st case study, case study 1, first project, intelligent search, search platform, knowledge search, search product, ai search, search engine, copilot search, connectors, connector management, search

**Overview**

> **Intelligent Enterprise Search Platform (UnifyApps)**
>
> An AI-powered search platform that connects all of a company's tools into one shared knowledge index, so people get **direct answers with sources** instead of searching system after system.
>
> - **Role:** Lead Product Designer, end to end from client brief to developer handoff
> - **Platform:** Web and mobile
> - **Timeline:** 2025 – present
> - **Core idea:** in enterprise AI, trust is the product
>
> [Read the case study →](https://vishnuprasad.space/work/enterprise-search)

**Problem**

> Organisations had the information they needed, but it was scattered across documents, chat tools and business apps. Five problems stood out:
>
> 1. Search returned **files, not answers**
> 2. Knowledge sat in **disconnected systems** with no single place to manage it
> 3. Results **ignored who was asking** and their role
> 4. Teams **couldn't customise** the experience without engineering
> 5. Users **couldn't see how their data was handled**, which hurt trust

**Users**

> The platform serves three roles, each with a different need:
>
> - **Employees** want clear, trustworthy answers with sources
> - **Administrators** want to manage integrations, permissions and settings without engineering help
> - **Compliance teams** want to see what data the platform can access, how it's used, and audit it
>
> The product team defined these roles and led the research; their findings shaped every design decision.

**Features**

> Five core capabilities, each solving one of the five problems:
>
> - **Connect Applications:** Slack, Jira, GitHub, Google Drive and more in one knowledge base, with clear connection statuses
> - **Manage Connections:** admins see what's syncing, choose what's included and see failures
> - **Personalised Results:** direct, role-aware answers with sources instead of file lists
> - **Customise Enterprise Search:** branding, wording, enabled tools and AI model through plain-language settings
> - **Agent Marketplace:** ready-made agents for finance, legal, sales and operations, each showing what it does and whether it's verified

**Approach**

> The platform works as a **copilot**: Search, Chat, Agents, Spaces and Apps share one index and one interaction model. Responsibility grows across three levels: **Ask → Configure → Govern**.

**Decisions & trade-offs**

> From the case study:
>
> - **A marketplace instead of only an agent builder**, so successful agents can be reused
> - **Sync failures and unverified agents are shown openly**, to build trust

**Outcomes**

> These are **UnifyApps' published figures** from a large financial institution's deployment; Vishnu did not measure them himself:
>
> - ~**$5.5M** projected annual cost savings
> - **800K+** consents captured in the first two weeks
> - **90% faster** time-to-market (two weeks vs. a typical 12–18 months)
> - **37%** better message delivery after consolidating 27+ messaging systems

**How AI is used**

> AI in the search platform:
>
> - **Retrieval with citations:** answers grounded in connected sources, with the source shown
> - **Role-aware results:** answers shaped by who is asking
> - **Agents:** a marketplace of verified, ready-made agents
> - **Configurable models:** admins choose the AI model in plain-language settings

**Learnings**

> Reflections from the project:
>
> - **Trust drives adoption** more than AI performance does
> - **Sources with every answer** make verification easy
> - **Transparency about failures** and pending states builds confidence
> - Enterprise AI must be designed for **admins and compliance reviewers**, not only end users

**His role**

> Vishnu was the **Lead Product Designer**, owning design end to end from client brief to developer handoff, with ICD as design partner to UnifyApps. The product team defined the roles and led the research. Timeline: 2025 – present, web and mobile.

**Overview**

> **Committee Board Agentic Application (DGE / ADEO, Abu Dhabi)**
>
> A government platform that helps the Abu Dhabi Executive Office's committees **analyse, deliberate on, vote on and record decisions** on 1,000+ high-impact proposals a month.
>
> - **Client:** UnifyApps for DGE and ADEO
> - **Partners:** Frog Design and Nagarro Design (ICD as design partner)
> - **Platform:** Tablet-first, plus web
> - **Timeline:** April 2025 – February 2026
> - **Role:** Lead Product Designer
>
> [Read the case study →](https://vishnuprasad.space/work/adeo)

**Problem**

> ADEO handles **1,000+ high-impact submissions every month**, mostly Arabic PDFs and presentations. Preparation could take **up to 15 working days**. Four structural problems:
>
> 1. **Slow preparation:** members skimmed documents right before sessions
> 2. **Unsearchable history:** five years of decisions (about 3,000–4,000 documents) couldn't be used during deliberation
> 3. **Absent members had no voice:** no way to contribute or record a position
> 4. **Incomplete records:** minutes, resolutions and action items were scattered

**Users**

> Three user types:
>
> - **Committee members:** about **15 minutes per topic**, so they need structured briefings
> - **Secretaries:** own the long preparation and documentation around each session
> - **Observers and admins:** need visibility without the ability to act
>
> Research was continuous: requirements came directly from the client and departments, validated at each major decision.

**Features**

> The core flow is **Analyse → Deliberate → Decide & Record**:
>
> - **Session agenda:** members see only their committees, active session on top, proposals split into *For Information* and *Decision Making*
> - **AI analysis and briefing:** a summary, strategic alignment score, ROI estimate, feasibility rating and risk assessment for every proposal, shown before the document
> - **AI assistant:** searches the five-year archive from inside the session
> - **Voting and async participation:** five outcomes, each requiring a rationale; absent members can vote and give feedback
> - **Live documentation:** minutes, action items and resolution drafts generated during the session

**Approach**

> Vishnu structured the product around how committees actually work: **Analyse → Deliberate → Decide & Record**.
>
> - **Briefing first:** members have ~15 minutes per topic, so the AI briefing appears before the raw document
> - **Scoped arrival:** each member sees only their own committees
> - **Deliberate voting:** every vote needs a rationale
> - **Records as a by-product:** documentation is generated live, so nothing is drafted after the session
> - **Tablet-first:** the proposal stays central and the assistant is collapsed by default

**Decisions & trade-offs**

> Every decision came with a named cost:
>
> - **Scoping members to their own committees** keeps arrival focused, but multi-committee members need a separate view
> - **AI content first** speeds understanding, but trust has to be earned immediately
> - **Mandatory voting rationale** makes voting deliberately slower; that friction is right for government decisions
> - **Proposal over assistant on tablet:** the assistant is collapsed by default to protect focus

**Outcomes**

> Impact as reported in the case study:
>
> - **Preparation:** up to 15 working days → **minutes**, via AI-generated briefings
> - **History:** 3,000–4,000 documents across 5 years became **queryable mid-session**
> - **Scale:** **1,000+ proposals a month** analysed, deliberated, voted on and documented in one platform
> - **Documentation:** minutes, action items and resolution drafts generated **live**

**How AI is used**

> AI in ADEO:
>
> - **Proposal briefings:** summary, strategic alignment score, ROI estimate, feasibility rating and risk assessment
> - **Archive assistant:** conversational search across five years of decisions
> - **Live documentation:** minutes, action items and resolution drafts generated during the session
> - **Clear labelling:** AI content is always marked, and humans approve before anything becomes an official record

**Learnings**

> Reflections:
>
> - **Label AI clearly** so people always know what was machine-generated
> - **Important decisions deserve deliberate interactions**, not speed
> - **People stay in control:** AI drafts; humans review, edit and approve before anything becomes official

**His role**

> Vishnu was the **Lead Product Designer**, working through UnifyApps for DGE and ADEO alongside **Frog Design** and **Nagarro Design**, with ICD as design partner. April 2025 – February 2026, tablet-first with web.

**Voting**

> Voting is deliberately structured:
>
> - **Five outcomes:** Approve, Approve with Comments, Not Approved, Return for Committee, Abstain
> - **A rationale is required** for every vote, creating a record of why each decision was made
> - **Absent members** can vote asynchronously and give feedback across four dimensions
>
> The extra friction is intentional: government decisions should be considered, not rushed.

**Overview**

>

>

> - **Timeline:** 2025 – present
> - **Goal:** make AI easy to adopt, govern and scale across roles
>

**Problem**

> Getting from an idea to a working agent took too many disconnected steps:
>
> 1. **Product managers** couldn't configure or launch agents without engineering
> 2. **Workflow logic** lived in separate documents that drifted out of sync with the product
> 3. **Compliance and security checks** came after development, causing rework
> 4. **Knowledge** was scattered, so quality and freshness were hard to manage
> 5. **No way to evaluate** an agent before deployment

**Users**

> Three roles:
>
> - **Product managers:** define use cases and behaviour; need to act without engineering
> - **Engineers:** need precise control over prompts, models and testing, in a focused workspace
> - **Compliance reviewers:** need visibility, audit history and security controls before approval

**Features**

> - **Simple Agent:** plain-language setup of role, goal and knowledge, with no model selection or prompt engineering
> - **Workflow Agent:** a visual canvas of triggers, conditions and actions, with execution traces non-engineers can verify
> - **Advanced Agent:** full control over models, retrieval, guardrails and evaluations, with each decision isolated and testable
> - **Guardrails & Observability:** guardrails and PII rules set during configuration; every interaction traced
> - **Reusable Skills:** a shared library (web search, file analysis, compliance checks, API actions) any agent can call

**Approach**

> The platform is structured as **Configure → Orchestrate → Tune**, with Simple, Workflow and Advanced modes under one design system. From the case study: "Simplicity means showing each user the right level of complexity, not removing features."

**Decisions & trade-offs**

> Trade-offs he named explicitly:
>
> - **Simple mode hides technical controls**, so engineers may find it limiting; Advanced mode exists for them
> - **Advanced mode's depth** is deliberately not aimed at people who don't need it
> - **Guardrails come first**, so reviewers check documented decisions instead of retrofitting
> - **Traces show gaps openly**, which looks less polished but avoids confident wrong answers
> - **Shared skills** are harder to maintain than purpose-built ones, but they stop teams rebuilding the same capability

**Outcomes**

> These are **UnifyApps' published figures** from a large financial institution's deployment; Vishnu did not measure them himself:
>
> - ~**$5.5M** projected annual cost savings
> - **800K+** consumer consents captured in two weeks
> - **90% faster** time-to-market
> - **37%** better message delivery after consolidating 27+ messaging systems

**How AI is used**

> The whole product is about AI agents:
>
> - Three build modes for different skill levels
> - **Guardrails and PII rules** set at configuration time
> - **Execution traces** of every tool call, retrieval and model decision
> - **Evaluations** before deployment
> - **Reusable skills** shared across agents

**Learnings**

> Reflections:
>
> - **Simplicity** is showing each user the right level of complexity
> - **Governance** works best when it's built into the workflow from the start
> - **Role-specific experiences** help each user focus and adopt faster

<Section label="Engagement" title="Unifying enterprise knowledge into one intelligent experience">

We led the end-to-end product design of an AI-powered enterprise search platform — built to be deployed and sold across large organisations — working directly from the client brief through to Final-for-Dev handoff, representing ICD as the design partner for UnifyApps. The platform replaces fragmented tool-switching with a single intelligent interface, connecting search, agents, integrations, and administration on a shared knowledge index that works from the same understanding of who each user is and what they have access to.


</Section>

<Section label="The Problem" title="Good intentions. Broken handoffs.">

Organizations already had the information they needed, but it was scattered across documents, communication tools, and business applications. Finding the right answer meant searching multiple systems, checking permissions, and piecing information together manually. The challenge wasn't access to data—it was making that knowledge easy to discover, trust, and use.

<Points>
  <Point title="Search returned files instead of answers.">Users received a list of documents rather than a direct answer. They still had to open multiple files, compare information, and find the right context themselves.</Point>
  <Point title="Knowledge was spread across disconnected systems.">Information lived in different applications with no single place to manage or understand what AI could access. This made knowledge difficult to maintain and trust.</Point>
  <Point title="Results treat everyone the same">Search has no understanding of who is asking or what they need. Everyone gets the same results regardless of their role or context.</Point>
  <Point title="Teams couldn't adapt the platform to their needs.">Different teams had different workflows, but the experience remained the same for everyone. Customizing it required engineering effort, limiting flexibility and adoption.</Point>
  <Point title="Limited transparency reduced trust.">When teams have no way to see how a system handles their data, they can't trust it enough to use it.</Point>
</Points>

</Section>

<Section stack label="Discovery" title="Three roles. Three different expectations.">

The product team defined the roles and led the research. The brief gave us three working roles — we turned those findings into clear design decisions for each.

<Points>
  <Point title="Employees needed answers they could trust.">Users wanted more than fast search results. They needed clear, reliable answers with supporting sources so they could act confidently without manually verifying everything.</Point>
  <Point title="Administrators needed control without engineering.">Administrators understood their organization's needs but couldn't configure the platform on their own. They needed the ability to manage integrations, permissions, and settings without depending on engineering teams.</Point>
  <Point title="Compliance teams needed visibility.">Compliance reviewers needed to understand what data the platform could access and how it was being used. Clear permissions, auditability, and transparency made approvals faster and more reliable.</Point>
</Points>

Together, these insights shaped our strategy: build a platform that delivers trusted answers for employees, gives administrators control, and provides compliance teams with the visibility they need to confidently approve adoption.

<Section label="Design Strategy" title="One platform. Different levels of responsibility.">

We designed the platform as a copilot: Search, Chat, Agents, Spaces, and Apps working as one system, on a shared knowledge index and a single interaction model. The same product then opens up as responsibility grows — from asking a question, to configuring how the product behaves, to governing what it can reach. Nobody is asked to trust a system they cannot see inside.

<Pillars>
  <Pillar title="Ask">Search in natural language and receive clear answers with supporting sources. Designed for users who simply want to find information and take action.</Pillar>
  <Pillar title="Configure">Connect data sources, manage integrations, and customize the platform without relying on engineering. Designed for administrators responsible for maintaining the experience.</Pillar>
  <Pillar title="Govern">See what the system reaches, set its boundary, approve on evidence.</Pillar>
</Pillars>

<Feature title="Connect Applications" lead="One place to bring in everything the organisation knows." images={["es-connect-1", "es-connect-2"]}>

Search can only answer from the knowledge it can access. By connecting tools like Slack, Jira, GitHub, and Google Drive, teams can create a single shared knowledge base without depending on IT. Clear connection statuses show exactly what data is available, making the platform transparent and easier to trust.

</Feature>

<Feature title="Manage Connections" lead="The place where someone stays responsible for the data." images={["es-manage-1", "es-manage-2"]}>

Connecting a source is easy. Keeping it right is the work. Here, administrators check that data is still updating, choose what should and should not be included, and see clearly what has failed. It turns the knowledge base from something assumed to be correct into something anyone can check — which is the difference between a system people trust and one they quietly stop using.


</Feature>

<Feature title="Customise Enterprise Search" lead="The controls that let a company make the product its own." images={["es-customise-1", "es-customise-2", "es-customise-3"]}>

Every organisation that buys the platform works differently, so the product has to feel like theirs rather than ours. Administrators set how search looks, sounds, and behaves — the branding, the wording, the tools that are switched on, the AI model behind it — on their own, without engineering. The settings are written in plain language, so the person who knows what the organisation needs is the same person who can go and change it.

</Feature>

<Feature title="Agent Marketplace" lead="Where finding an answer turns into getting the work done." images={["es-marketplace"]}>

Search answers a question. An agent does the task. This is where teams find ready-made agents for finance, legal, sales or operations and put them straight to work. Making it a store rather than a builder was the point: what one team proves useful, every team can reuse. Each agent shows what it does and whether it has been checked — because the hard part is never finding an agent, it is being allowed to trust one.

</Feature>

<Section label="Reflections" title="In enterprise AI, trust is the product.">

Building this platform showed me that AI performance alone isn't enough. People need to understand, verify, and rely on the system before they'll use it in their daily work. The biggest design challenge wasn't creating AI—it was designing experiences that people could confidently trust.

<Points>
  <Point title="Trust starts with every answer.">Users trust AI more when they can immediately see where an answer came from. Showing sources and context alongside each response makes information easier to verify and reduces the need for manual checking.</Point>
  <Point title="Transparency builds confidence.">A trustworthy product doesn't hide problems. Showing failed syncs, pending connections, or unverified agents helps users understand the system's current state and gives them confidence that nothing is being hidden.</Point>
  <Point title="Design for everyone involved in the decision.">The people using a product aren't always the ones deciding whether it gets adopted. Administrators and compliance teams also need visibility and confidence in how the system works. Designing for all stakeholders is essential for enterprise AI adoption.</Point>
</Points>
